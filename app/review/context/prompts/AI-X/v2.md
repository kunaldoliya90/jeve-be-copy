## Your task: group these exceptions by cause

You are given the checks whose exceptions are too numerous to read as a list: each one's
total exception count, and the figures behind it with their counts, money impact and a few
example rows.

Return `clusters`. For each cause give:

- `cause`: one line describing what the affected rows have in common.
- `affected`: how many rows that cause accounts for.
- `financial_impact`: the amount it accounts for, as a string, or null if not monetary.
- `figure_ids`: the figures it was drawn from.
- `example_rows`: a few row numbers a reviewer can open.

Rules specific to this step:

1. **Every exception belongs to exactly one cause, and the counts must reconcile** to the
   totals you were given. They are checked, and a set of causes that does not add up is
   reported to the reader as incomplete.
2. **Describe, do not diagnose.** "312 rows where earned leave is a hardcoded zero" is a
   cause. "The payroll team used the wrong extract" is a guess about people you cannot see.
3. **Cite only figure ids you were given.** A cluster citing anything else is discarded.
4. **Change nothing.** Statuses, counts and amounts were decided before you were called, and
   nothing you write can alter one. You are making a long list readable.
5. **Fewer causes is better, but never at the cost of a true one.** Two distinct problems
   merged into one line costs the reader the second problem.
